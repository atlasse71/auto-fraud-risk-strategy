# Fraud Typology Control Matrix: Auto Loan Underwriting

This matrix maps each first-party and third-party fraud typology to the signals that detect it, the controls in the LOS decision engine, and the residual risk owner. Every control listed is implemented in `config/decision_rules.yaml` or in the ML model features (`src/fraudlab/model.py`).

The goal is to **stop fraud before funding**. Post-funding detection (FPD, early-payment default) is used to label outcomes and tune controls. It is not the primary defence.

| Typology | How it shows up in auto lending | Key signals | Preventive controls (pre-funding) | Rule IDs | Residual risk & owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Synthetic identity** | A manufactured identity (real SSN fragment combined with a fake name and DOB) is "cultivated" over 12–24 months through authorized-user piggy-backing. It then busts out on a high-LTV auto loan. | SSN issued in adulthood relative to DOB; tradeline age that doesn't fit the SSN age; ≥3 AU tradelines on young credit; new phone and email; phone shared across identities; SSN velocity; "too good" bureau score on a thin file | Vendor B synthetic score (decline ≥ 800); AU piggy-back rule; SSN-age and contact-tenure rule; phone link analysis; SSN velocity; ML features `credit_age_vs_ssn`, `au_share` | SYN-001…004, VEL-002 | Sophisticated synthetics with organic history. **Mitigation:** eCBSV SSN verification, consortium identity graph (Vendor E POC). Owner: Fraud Strategy |
| **Straw buyer** | A creditworthy front person buys the vehicle for someone who couldn't qualify, such as a family member or a trafficking or export ring. The dealer is often complicit. | Buyer–dealer distance; vehicle price relative to income for young buyers; co-buyer and device mismatch; dealer fraud concentration; repeated dealer re-submissions; consortium links between buyer and other borrowers | Distance + high-value rule; affordability-mismatch rule; dealer target-encoded risk in the ML model; **dealer watchlist** (z-score) feeding enhanced review or suspension; stips for proof of residence and insurance in the buyer's name | STR-001, STR-002, dealer watchlist | **Weakest-captured typology.** Bureau data cannot see it. **Mitigation:** Vendor E consortium graph (largest POC lift of any typology), dealer audits, post-funding title and registration checks. | **Income misrepresentation** | Stated income is inflated, by the applicant or by the dealer "re-keying" the deal until it approves. | **Application fraud / identity theft** | A stolen identity is used to originate the loan. The vehicle is moved or exported quickly. ## Decision hierarchy

`DECLINE` (hard stop with fraud adverse-action reason) **>** `REVIEW` (human analyst, 4-hour SLA) **>** `STIP` (automated condition such as mandatory bank-link, POI or POR) **>** `APPROVE`.

Soft signals with low standalone precision should be **features in the ML score or risk-tiered STIPs**, not manual-review triggers. This principle drives the planned rule retirements (ID-003, STR-002, INC-004) flagged in the monthly report.

## Customer-friction guardrails

- Auto-approval floor: **≥ 80%** of credit-approved applications (dealer experience).
- False-positive rate ceiling: **≤ 15%** of good applicants routed away from auto-approval.
- Thin-file and no-file applicants (a core Lendbuzz segment) **must not** be penalised for having no bureau data. In the ML model, missing bureau fields are treated as information, not as risk. The fair-lending review in `MODEL_GOVERNANCE.md` checks segment-level false-positive parity.
